# World Tension v2 — on-device headline assessment

Version 3 ([world-tension-v3.md](world-tension-v3.md)) blends the day's assessment below with the week's history for the reading the sheet shows; the assessment itself is unchanged.

v2 replaces the displayed v1 formula with an AI judgment of global stability from current international headlines. The AI chooses a 0–100 reading through reasoning; factor scores are not multiplied or summed. Bands are Zen (0–19), Mild (20–39), Warm (40–59), Hot (60–79) and Boiling (80–100). These labels describe an interpretation of the supplied news, not a measured probability of catastrophe.

## Evidence and factors

The existing opt-in twelve-feed international English panel supplies dated headlines from the preceding 24 hours. Most panel feeds and regions must have reported, using the established coverage gate. Royal, celebrity, sport, entertainment and lifestyle headlines are left out, and within each region headlines with v1 conflict, pressure or disaster cues come first. One representative per clustered event is selected; the input is bounded to 12 current headlines balanced across publisher regions. Up to 8 headlines from the preceding six days in the same existing event clusters supply comparable novelty context. Unrelated previous events are excluded. Missing or unrelated prior context is explicitly identified, never treated as proof every event is new. Publication date alone does not establish an escalation. Coverage, selection bounds and regional gaps limit the claim to the available panel.

The on-device model evaluates all four factors together:

- **Novelty:** new crises or escalations matter more than ongoing situations already priced in. Compare current and previous headlines; do not confuse repeated coverage with escalation.
- **Proximity to worst case:** evidenced proximity to catastrophic outcomes, especially nuclear/WMD situations, has outsized importance. Threats and hypothetical outcomes are not actual attacks.
- **Reversibility:** irreversible invasions and loss of life matter more than reversible sanctions and rhetoric.
- **Scope of impact:** global systemic risk, economic contagion and alliance triggers matter more than localized incidents. Numerous violent or disaster headlines alone do not justify the highest band.

## Explanation and acceptance

The model reasons about global scope first, then worst-case proximity, novelty and reversibility, and chooses the number last. It returns a labelled report (lines in any order; Markdown, list markers and introductions are ignored), converted to a structured assessment: score, a lead sentence naming the main development, a short summary, exactly four named rationales, the IDs of three to five driving headlines and the complete supplied headline snapshot, disclosed by the app. Native guided generation refused even a peaceful control on the tested model, so this uses the existing plain-text generation seam with a strict parser. Score range, paragraph lengths and all four distinct factors are checked. Previous headlines contextualize novelty and must not be described as current events. Per-factor generated citations are intentionally not shown: the tested model assigned incorrect IDs, so support is checked against the complete supplied snapshot instead. For the same reason a driving headline the model names is shown only when the explanation shares a word with it; otherwise the sheet ranks current headlines by their v1 cues.

Grounding is deterministic: every figure in the lead, summary and rationales must occur in the supplied headlines, and at least three in four capitalised names (not at a sentence start; all but one when there are few) must occur in them, so a country inferred from its city's headline passes while a cast of invented places does not; compass and continent words are generic; demonyms and inflections of headline words count as present. Risk interpretations and absence-of-evidence limits are abstract and pass. A second model session previously judged support with a one-word YES/NO answer; it rejected supported abstract reasoning so often that no assessment was shown, and was removed. A rejected draft gets at most one conservative retry. Every retried draft passes the same format and grounding checks. If it still fails, no score is saved and the previous accepted assessment keeps its own date. This grounding remains a heuristic; it does not make publisher claims or AI reasoning infallible. Generated reasoning is labelled as assessed on device. The UI supplies numeric reading/band and any comparison from accepted assessments; the model is not asked to invent an average or daily change.

## Policy, caching and history

AI and panel collection must be enabled before any cache is read. Low Power Mode and thermal pressure defer assessment. When the on-device model cannot run or its drafts fail validation and no assessment exists for the current UTC day, the sheet shows a keyword estimate: fixed weights on the v1 deterministic cues of the current headlines, halved so a typical day of conflict reports reads Warm rather than Hot, labelled as an estimate and never as AI, and never saved. When an accepted assessment exists for the day, it stays and the failure is reported beside it.

Inputs, UTC day and macOS model generation identify cached assessments. Concurrent identical requests share generation; cancelled callers do not publish or persist their answer. Reassess bypasses the completed cache. All processing uses the existing on-device model seam, fresh sessions and greedy sampling; nothing is sent to cloud AI.

Accepted history is stored atomically beside the SQLite library in `news.sqlite3.world-tension-v2.json`, separately from v1. It retains one assessment per UTC day, at most 30 recorded days. Clearing AI data or all local caches clears this derived history. Gaps remain gaps; different model generations are not connected or compared. Original v1 calibration and corpus data are preserved, not backfilled or relabelled.
